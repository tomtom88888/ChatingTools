DITTO - BUSINESS PLAN
Status: backlog. The app is for personal use for now; this is the plan for
if and when it goes public.
Last updated: 29 Sep 2026


1. THE PRODUCT, IN ONE LINE

Ditto writes replies that sound like you, learned from your own chat
history. Main audience: dating. People texting a match who want a reply
that is good AND sounds like them, not like a chatbot.


2. MODEL: FREEMIUM

Free
  - Writing replies (the core feature).
  - Chat data (reply times, word counts, who talks more). Fun to share,
    and shared screenshots are free advertising.
  - Limit: 10 replies a day (hard lock).
  - Model: Luna for everyone, if Luna turns out good enough (see 4).
    If it isn't: the first 5 replies of the day on Terra, the rest on Luna.
  - Imports: 1-2 chats. That's also a natural reason to upgrade ("add
    more people").
  - Remember: one free run on a single chat, or a 3-day premium trial,
    so people feel the difference (see 5).

Premium
  - Unlimited replies, all on Terra.
  - Remember: facts about the other person feed into every reply
    ("her exam is Friday", "his dog is called Biscuit"). This is the
    headline feature: replies that are clearly better than free.
  - Extras: Search and Chat groupings.
  - Unlimited imports.
  - "Unlimited" has a quiet fair-use cap (around 200 replies a day) that
    no real person reaches. It protects against scripts and people
    reselling access. The store listing can still say unlimited.

Why this split
  - People pay for better replies, not for charts. Remember is what makes
    replies better, so it leads premium. Search and groupings are nice
    to explore but rarely the reason someone subscribes.
  - A daily limit tends to convert better than a locked feature: people
    hit it at the moment they need a reply.


3. WHAT COUNTS AS ONE "REPLY"

  - One tap of "Write replies" = 1 reply, even though it makes several
    drafts (the options plus the change-of-topic one).
  - Tweaks (Shorter / Warmer / More like me) and "Try again": free users
    get one free tweak per reply so they can still get it right; after
    that, each counts as a reply. Premium: unlimited.
  - Reading a screenshot is a separate, pricier call. It stays on the good
    model for everyone: a misread screenshot ruins the reply however good
    the writing model is.


4. BEFORE DECIDING LUNA VS TERRA FOR FREE USERS

Test it on yourself first, with what the app already has:
  - Settings > reply model: use gpt-5.6-luna for a week, then
    gpt-5.6-terra for a week.
  - Chat data > "How the suggestions have done" logs how often a
    suggestion is copied. Compare the two weeks.
  - If Luna's copy rate is close to Terra's: everyone gets Luna on free.
    If it's clearly worse: first 5 a day on Terra, then Luna.
  - Check current prices for both before deciding; the gap decides how
    generous free can be.
  - Risk to keep in mind: if free replies don't sound like the user, they
    quit before ever seeing premium. Free has to be good enough to hook.


5. LETTING PEOPLE TRY REMEMBER

Remember is premium's best feature, but its value is only visible once
used. Options (pick one):
  - A 3-day free trial of premium.
  - One free Remember run on one chat, with its facts used in replies to
    that person, so the free user sees the difference in reply quality.


6. COSTS TO PLAN FOR

  - Replies: cheap per reply. The bulk of the bill at scale.
  - Screenshot reading: pricier per call than a reply; one per reply.
  - Importing a chat: embedding all its replies. Cheap per chat, but paid
    before the user has replied even once. That's why free imports are
    capped.
  - Remember: several calls per chat (up to 9 on a long chat). Premium
    only, occasional.
  - Groupings: one naming call per run. Premium only, occasional.
  - Search: one small embedding call per search. Negligible.


7. WHAT HAS TO BE BUILT (IN ORDER)

  0. Validate first: give the APK to 10-20 friends for two weeks and
     check the copy rate and whether they keep coming back. If they
     don't, no pricing fixes that. Don't build payments before this.
  1. Backend.
     - Accounts (sign in with Google / Apple).
     - All OpenAI calls move to the server; users never see an API key.
     - Per-account daily limits and usage metering (see 2 and 3).
     - Chat text now passes through the server, so the "never leaves this
       phone" line changes to an honest privacy policy. Keep storing
       chats on the phone; the server only relays requests and keeps
       nothing it doesn't need.
  2. Subscriptions.
     - Google Play Billing and Apple in-app purchases are required for
       digital features. RevenueCat handles both stores.
     - Premium is checked on the server, never only in the app: a check
       in the app is trivial to bypass, especially in a sideloaded APK.
  3. App store release.
     - Our own signing key (replace the repo's sideload key).
     - Privacy policy and data-safety forms. Dating + AI + other people's
       messages will get a close look in review; be explicit about what
       is sent to OpenAI and why.
     - Store listing, screenshots, age rating.


8. PRICING

  - Apps that write dating messages usually charge weekly or monthly,
    with a short free trial.
  - Price on what it's worth to the user (a better reply at the moment it
    matters), not on API cost, which is a small fraction of any sensible
    price.
  - Decide weekly vs monthly after seeing trial-to-paid numbers.


9. RISKS

  - Big platforms (keyboards, the messaging apps' own assistants) add
    "reply in your style" themselves. Our edge: learning from your own
    history, and Remember.
  - Privacy: other people's messages go to OpenAI. Must be disclosed
    clearly; some users will say no.
  - The workflow (export, then screenshot per reply) is heavier than an
    inline keyboard. Worth revisiting before a public launch.
  - Early dating chats happen in Hinge, Tinder and Bumble, not WhatsApp.
    Screenshots from those apps work for replies, but the screenshot
    reader is only tuned for WhatsApp so far.
